# Original Game Direction and MVP Contract

## Working direction — Snatch a Snack
An original social collection game in a colorful toy food court. Players spot expressive snack-creatures, choose an escape route past a readable guardian, and bank successful deliveries into a personal display. The screenshot supplies **mood**: bright floor, large focal creature, collectible silhouettes, compact side menus, and visible currencies. It does not establish the original game's mechanics or economy.

## Original differentiator
Prototype changing delivery routes and protected banked progress. Players contest current unbanked cargo or collaborate on deliveries; they do not permanently lose their stored collection. The route decision is the hook. A cooperative shortcut must also have a viable solo alternative.

## First-minute proposal
1. Spawn facing one reachable snack and the player's empty display.
2. A single contextual objective teaches grab.
3. A guardian telegraph makes the return tense but forgiving.
4. Deposit produces a short celebration and a visible display.
5. A free upgrade and the index reveal another useful goal.

These are design hypotheses, not measured time-to-fun guarantees. Test with fresh players before adding a large catalog.

## MVP scope
- One shared arena, two routes, one guardian family.
- Three snack families and a small set of authored rarity variants.
- Personal displays, coins, one useful upgrade axis, collection discoveries.
- Save/reconnect recovery, responsive HUD, first-time guidance, basic telemetry.
- Mock catalog first; a few guaranteed cosmetic products only after fulfilment tests.

## Explicitly later
Player trading, ranked queues, multiple places, paid randomness, subscriptions, complex prestige, and global economies. Each introduces a separate design or transaction problem and is not required to prove the main action is fun.

## Production contract
One authoritative record owns each item. Clients request intent and render responses. Purchases are durable and idempotent. All names, models, UI art, sounds, thumbnails, and maps are original or properly licensed. Normal play remains useful without purchases.

## Go / revise / stop
Go when fresh players understand the action and deliberately repeat a run. Revise when they like the picture but cannot find the goal or do not retry. Stop expanding content when the small prototype is not enjoyable. No revenue, virality, or platform recommendation is promised.
